N Scanning
Research prototype · not a medical device

Read a spine MRI slice, see what stands out, and know when the image is not good enough.

Phase one reads sagittal lumbar MRI. It outlines the vertebrae, discs and spinal canal, flags possible disc findings, compares each level with a reference range, and tells you what kind of scan to get when it cannot give a reliable reading. Everything runs in your browser; the image is never uploaded.

How it works

  1. Outline. A small U-Net segments vertebrae, discs and the spinal canal. It runs twice (image and mirror image); disagreement between the two runs is one of the signals for an unreliable result.
  2. Measure. For each disc level it measures disc height, canal depth and vertebral slip relative to the neighbouring vertebrae, and the front-to-back height ratio of each vertebral body, so the numbers do not depend on image scale.
  3. Compare. Each measurement is compared with discs that radiologists graded as unremarkable in the training data and reported as a robust z-score.
  4. Grade. On T2-weighted images a second model grades each disc for Pfirrmann degeneration, herniation, bulging, narrowing, slip, endplate and Modic changes. A finding is called likely, uncertain or unlikely using cut-offs fixed on held-out patients.
  5. Decide. If the image is too small, blurred, the wrong view or the anatomy outline is unstable, it shows no findings and explains what scan to get instead.

Data and credit

Trained on the SPIDER lumbar spine MRI dataset (van der Graaf et al., Scientific Data 2024; CC BY 4.0). Example images are derived from SPIDER under the same licence.

Limits

  • One 2D slice is not a full MRI study; radiologists read several sequences and planes.
  • The reference group is patients with back pain whose discs were graded unremarkable, not a healthy population, and it is not adjusted for age or sex yet.
  • Level names assume the lowest disc is L5/S1. Transitional vertebrae, implants, tumours, infection and fractures were not part of training.
